Introducing Aurora: a new frontier model family

October 12, 20266 minute read

Today we're introducing Aurora, our first family of models trained on our new research stack. Aurora is available starting today through our API and the Aurora Code assistant, with a broader rollout in our consumer apps over the coming weeks.

This post walks through how we scaled training, what the models can do, how well those abilities carry over to new kinds of tasks, and how we approached safety.

01Scaling with intention

Over the past year we rebuilt our pretraining, post-training and inference systems from the ground up. The goal was simple: every additional unit of compute should buy a predictable gain in capability.

Much of that work happened on Helios, our new training cluster, which came online this spring.

Deep domain expertise

We worked with specialists in medicine, law, finance and the sciences to build evaluation suites that reflect real professional work rather than trivia.

Active Memory

Aurora can choose what to remember across a long session and recall it when it matters, so it stays coherent over hours-long tasks without stuffing everything into context.

Active Focus

For long inputs, the model learns where to spend attention. In practice that means faster responses on huge documents with no loss in accuracy.

03Capabilities that generalize

A model that only does well on tasks resembling its training data is brittle. We measure the gap between in-distribution and out-of-distribution performance, $\Delta = \text{acc}_{\text{ID}} - \text{acc}_{\text{OOD}}$, and train specifically to shrink it.

04Safety at the frontier

Each Aurora model went through red-teaming for privacy, adversarial robustness and misuse before release. We ran evaluations both in-house and with outside experts, and we'll keep publishing what we learn.

  • Privacy: memory features are opt-in, and users can see and delete what is stored.
  • Robustness: we train against prompt-injection and jailbreak patterns found by automated red teams.
  • Misuse: layered classifiers and policy models monitor high-risk domains.
